The Evolution of Google Maps Street View Cameras: Gen 1 to Gen 4

Hardware GeoGuessr · 5 min read
March 2026

When Google launched Street View back in 2007, the cameras strapped to the roofs of those first Chevy Cobalts were surprisingly primitive. Over the last two decades, Google has completely overhauled their roof rigs four times.

If you play GeoGuessr or frequently look at historical Street View imagery, knowing which camera took a photo is one of the easiest ways to date a location at a glance. Here is how each generation looks and how you can spot them.

GEN 1 The Blurry Era

2007 – 2008

Gen 1 imagery looks like it was recorded on an old webcam through a dirty fishbowl. Google bought off-the-shelf commercial sensors for their earliest fleet. The photos have washed-out white skies, severe compression artifacts, and noticeable stitch tears where the images meet.

Where to find it: Gen 1 is very rare today because Google has re-driven almost everywhere. You can still occasionally find it on older, untouched highways in the US, Australia, and New Zealand.

GEN 2 The "Purple Halo" Era

2008 – 2009

Gen 2 cameras brought a noticeable jump in clarity and resolution, but they came with a famous quirk: looking up at the sun or down at the car reveals a distinct circular ring of purple or reddish chromatic aberration (known as the "halo").

It was a big step up from Gen 1, but the cameras still struggled with direct overhead sunlight and dark shadows.

GEN 3 The Workhorse

2009 – 2017

Generation 3 is what most people picture when they think of Google Street View. Google built a custom 15-lens rosette rig that snapped 75-megapixel panoramas. Skies turned bright blue, stitching lines disappeared, and road signs became easy to read from down the street.

Because Google expanded aggressively across Europe, Latin America, and Asia during these years, Gen 3 remains the most common imagery you will encounter in rural parts of the world.

GEN 4 Modern HD & LiDAR

2017 – Present

In 2017, Google replaced the bulky 15-lens ball with a streamlined rig featuring 7 high-resolution cameras totaling 140 megapixels. The colors are much more natural, details in bright sunlight and dark shadows stay clear, and the cars have LiDAR sensors to map 3D road depth.

If the road looks ultra-crisp, the sky is vivid, and the car's roof rack has a slim profile with a blue trim, you are looking at Gen 4.

Quick Comparison Table

Generation Years Used Resolution Telltale Signs
Gen 1 2007 – 2008 Low-res Blurry, white blown-out skies, visible seams
Gen 2 2008 – 2009 Early HD Circular purple halo around the sun, bottom car blur
Gen 3 2009 – 2017 75 Megapixels Crisp blue skies, sharp road signs, 15-lens ball
Gen 4 2017 – Present 140 Megapixels Rich natural colors, sharp details, LiDAR depth
