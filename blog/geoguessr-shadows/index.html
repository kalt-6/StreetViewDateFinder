How GeoGuessr Players Use Shadows to Pinpoint Exact Locations

GeoGuessr Strategy Solar & Shadows · 5 min read
Published: March 2026

In competitive GeoGuessr, players are dropped into a random Google Street View panorama somewhere on Earth and tasked with identifying their precise coordinates. While beginners search for road markers, scripts, and landmarks, top players analyze something far more consistent: the shadows.

By assessing the direction, length, and angle of shadows cast by cars, utility poles, and tree canopies, elite players extract rich geographic and temporal data. Here is how shadow analysis works in open-source geolocation.

STEP 01 The Hemisphere Check

Latitude Clue

The most fundamental use of shadows is determining your hemisphere. Because the sun tracks along the equator, its relative arc across the sky flips depending on whether you stand north or south of it.

Northern Hemisphere

The sun sits primarily in the southern sky. Consequently, ground shadows point toward true North.

Southern Hemisphere

The sun sits primarily in the northern sky. Consequently, ground shadows point toward true South.

Car Nadir Meta: Align the Google Maps compass to North and look down at the Street View car's own shadow. If the car's shadow stretches north, you can instantly rule out southern nations like Australia, Argentina, and South Africa.

STEP 02 Estimating the Time of Day

Solar Angle Clue

Once your hemisphere is established, shadow orientation reveals the local time of day. Because the sun rises in the East and sets in the West, shadows move in the exact opposite direction.

Morning Window (Sunrise – Noon)

The sun rises in the East, stretching long shadows toward the West.

Afternoon Window (Noon – Sunset)

The sun descends in the West, casting shadows toward the East.

This timing is vital when combined with coverage metas: if you know Google's camera cars only operated during morning schedules in a particular territory, seeing a prominent western shadow confirms you are in that specific region.

STEP 03 Seasonal Shadow Lengths

Temporal Clue

Shadow length also indicates the capture season. During winter months, the sun remains low across the horizon, casting elongated shadows throughout the day. During summer, high solar noon casts short, compact shadows directly beneath objects.

Foliage Correlation: Cross-reference shadow length with foliage. Elongated shadows combined with leafless deciduous trees signal late autumn or winter, helping you narrow the capture month and identify the camera generation Google had deployed.
