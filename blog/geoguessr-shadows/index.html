How GeoGuessr Players Use Shadows to Pinpoint Locations

GeoGuessr Tips · 5 min read
March 2026

When you drop into a random round of GeoGuessr with no road signs and nothing but trees around, one of the first things experienced players check is the shadows on the ground.

Shadows tell you a surprising amount about where you are in the world. With just a quick glance at the compass and the car's shadow, you can immediately rule out half the planet.

TIP 1 The Hemisphere Check

North vs South

Because the sun tracks across the equator, its direction in the sky depends entirely on whether you are north or south of it:

Northern Hemisphere

The sun is in the southern sky, meaning shadows always point toward North.

Southern Hemisphere

The sun is in the northern sky, meaning shadows point toward South.

The Car Check: In Google Maps, point your compass needle straight North and look down at the Street View car's blur. If the shadow extends toward the top of your screen (North), you are in the Northern Hemisphere. If it points toward the bottom (South), you're in the Southern Hemisphere (like Australia, South Africa, or South America).

TIP 2 Morning vs. Afternoon

Time of Day

Since the sun rises in the East and sets in the West, shadows always move in reverse:

Morning (East Sun)

Shadows stretch out towards the West.

Afternoon (West Sun)

Shadows stretch out towards the East.

This helps when you are trying to match road orientation: if a road runs east-west and the shadows run parallel down the asphalt toward the west, you know it was driven early in the morning.

TIP 3 Shadow Length & Season

Sun Angle

The length of a shadow is determined by how high the sun is in the sky:

  • Long, stretched shadows: Either early morning, late afternoon, or winter (when the sun never climbs very high). If you see long shadows and bare, leafless trees, it is almost certainly a late autumn or winter drive.
  • Short, tight shadows: Midday in the summer, when the sun is directly overhead.

Quick Reference

Shadow Direction Sun Position What It Means
Points North South Northern Hemisphere
Points South North Southern Hemisphere
Points West East Morning capture
Points East West Afternoon capture
Very Long + Bare Trees Low on horizon Late autumn or winter
