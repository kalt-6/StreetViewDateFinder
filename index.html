How It Works

How does this tool find the exact time?

Whenever a Google Street View car drives down a street, its roof sensors record the exact GPS satellite time for every frame it snaps. But on Google Maps, Google rounds that timestamp down to just the month and year (like "May 2021") in the corner of your screen.

STEP 1

Extract the Panorama ID

Every Street View photo has a unique 22-character ID buried inside its URL. The tool reads that ID and asks Google's servers for the photo's approximate month, year, and GPS coordinates.

STEP 2

Run a Binary Search

Google's backend supports an internal filter called photoAge. By testing the midpoint of the month and checking whether Google returns the image, the tool splits the time window in half repeatedly. In about 20 quick pings, it narrows the window down to the exact second.

STEP 3

Convert to Local Time

Once we have the raw UNIX timestamp, the tool uses the GPS coordinates to look up the location's local timezone. It automatically factors in historical Daylight Saving Time so you get the true local time of the location when the car drove past.

Camera History

Google Street View Camera Generations

Google has used four main generations of camera rigs since launching Street View in 2007. If you play GeoGuessr or do photo research, you can usually tell roughly how old an image is just by looking at the picture quality.

Generation Years Used Camera Rig How to Spot It
Gen 1 2007 – 2008 Early commercial sensors Very blurry, washed-out white skies, and noticeable seams where images are stitched together. Mostly found on older US highway coverage.
Gen 2 2008 – 2009 Early HD upgrade Noticeably sharper than Gen 1, but has a recognizable circular purple/red "halo" artifact when looking toward the sun or down at the car.
Gen 3 2009 – 2017 15-lens rosette (75 MP) The classic Street View look. Crisp blue skies, readable street signs, and clean stitching. Still the most common coverage across rural areas worldwide.
Gen 4 2017 – Present 7 HD cameras (140 MP) + LiDAR Vibrant colors, high resolution, great dynamic range in shadows, and smooth 3D transitions when moving down the road.

Quick Guide

How to Use This Tool

You can find the exact timestamp of any location in four simple steps:

01

Find your location

Open Google Maps on your computer and drop the yellow Pegman into Street View at the spot you want to inspect.

02

Copy the link

Copy the URL from your browser's address bar or Google Maps share menu. Mobile links (maps.app.goo.gl) are automatically detected and expanded.

03

Paste and run

Paste the link into the box at the top of this page and click Extract Time (or hit Enter).

04

See the exact time

Within a few seconds, you'll see the exact local time the photo was snapped, your local equivalent time, and the camera model.

About

About This Project

Street View Date Finder is an open-source project created to help GeoGuessr players, researchers, and curious users find the hidden capture times behind Google Street View photos.

Google Maps has photographed millions of miles of roads over the last 15+ years. Being able to check the exact time an image was taken is incredibly useful for verifying dates, looking up historical changes in neighborhoods, and practicing competitive GeoGuessr.

The source code is open-source under the MIT license and hosted on GitHub. If you run into a bug or have an idea for an improvement, feel free to open an issue on the repository.
