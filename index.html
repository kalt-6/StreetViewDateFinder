Technical Protocol

How Does Second-Level Extraction Work?

Google Maps publicly prints only the month and year watermark in its user interface. However, Google's mapping database indexes every image with a high-precision UNIX epoch timestamp. Here is how our client-side engine queries it directly.

STAGE 01 // PARSE

Pano ID & Geographic Vector Resolution

The extractor parses the unique 22-character Base64 identifier from your URL. It queries Google's internal MapsJs RPC service to retrieve the baseline year, month, and exact decimal coordinates recorded by the vehicle's GPS receiver.

STAGE 02 // SEARCH

Binary Search Convergence Protocol

Google's backend supports a protobuf filter parameter called photoAge with startSeconds and endSeconds. By querying successive midpoints across a 31-day window, the algorithm isolates the exact capture second in approximately 21 requests.

STAGE 03 // ASTRONOMY

Timezone & Solar Angle Calculation

Once the UNIX timestamp is pinpointed, the engine queries the IANA timezone database for the geographic coordinate to adjust for historical Daylight Saving Time. It then applies NOAA solar equations to determine solar elevation, azimuth, and projected shadow vectors.

Hardware Archeology

Google Street View Camera Generations

Google has deployed four major generations of camera rigs on its vehicles since 2007. Identifying the visual hardware signature allows researchers and GeoGuessr competitors to verify timeframes at a glance.

Generation Deployment Era Sensor Rig Specs Visual Artifacts & Signatures
Gen 1 2007 – 2008 Commercial low-res CCDs Blown-out white skies, severe stitch misalignment, heavy JPEG artifacts.
Gen 2 2008 – 2009 Standard Definition upgrade Circular "halo" ring around the sun, characteristic nadir blur circle at car base.
Gen 3 2009 – 2017 15-lens rosette (75 Megapixels) Vibrant blue skies, readable street signs at distance, smooth horizontal stitching. Most widespread rural imagery.
Gen 4 2017 – Present 7 HD sensors (140 MP) + dual Velodyne LiDAR Ultra-crisp dynamic range, natural color grading, smooth 3D depth transitions, reduced vehicle roof reflection.

Investigative Methodology

Using Solar Elevation & Shadows for Chronolocation

In open-source intelligence (OSINT) investigations and GeoGuessr matches, shadows are immutable geometric evidence. When you know the exact second an image was captured, sun angles can corroborate or disprove claims.

01

Shadow Angle Verification

Shadows fall exactly 180° opposite of the sun's azimuth. If the tool indicates a solar azimuth of 135° (Southeast), all vertical telephone poles and buildings will cast shadows toward 315° (Northwest).

02

Solar Elevation & Shadow Length

The solar elevation angle determines shadow length through trigonometry: Length = Height / tan(Elevation). A low sun angle (below 20°) indicates early morning or late afternoon.

03

Cross-Referencing Satellite Imagery

Comparing street-level shadows against overhead satellite passes (e.g. Sentinel-2, Landsat, Planet) lets analysts confirm whether physical changes occurred before or after a documented date.

04

Weather & Atmospheric Matching

Corroborate historical METAR airport weather reports and barometric pressure data with visible cloud formations and ground wetness captured at the exact timestamp.

Open-Source Precision Geolocation

Street View Date Finder was created to provide investigators, geographers, educators, and gamers with transparent access to geospatial image metadata.

Google Street View is one of the most comprehensive photographic records of human civilization. However, compressing timestamp data into a broad "Month Year" label limits the utility of the archive for chronological fact-checking, event verification, and competitive geographical deduction.
